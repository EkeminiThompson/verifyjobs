Encountered a
fraudulent posting?

Reporting job scams helps protect others from falling victim. Here's exactly what to do right now, and where to report it in your country.

!
If you've already sent money or shared bank details, act now

Contact your bank or mobile money provider immediately to freeze or reverse any transfers. Time is critical — the sooner you call, the better the chance of recovery. This is especially common with advance-fee / pay-before-you-start scams.

What to do right now
1
Stop all contact with the scammer

Block their number and email — but take screenshots first before blocking. Do not pay any further “fees.”

2
Screenshot everything

Save all messages, job postings, emails, payment requests, and any profile information as evidence for reporting. If the offer arrived on WhatsApp, keep the chat thread intact.

3
Contact your bank immediately

If money was sent, call your bank's fraud department right away. Ask to reverse or freeze the transfer. Credit cards often allow chargebacks; wire and crypto are harder.

4
Change compromised passwords

Update any passwords you shared and enable two-factor authentication on all important accounts.

5
Report to the authorities

Use the official links below for your country. Reports help law enforcement track patterns and prevent future victims.

Protect yourself going forward
✓
Always verify the company independently through their official website before applying. Use our 7-step verification guide.
✓
Never pay money for a job opportunity — training, equipment, or starter kits are all scam signals. Details: advance-fee job fraud.
✓
Call the official company phone number to confirm the offer and the recruiter's identity.
✓
Know the 15 warning signs of a job scam before you apply.
✕
Never share BVN, NIN, passport, or banking details with unsolicited recruiters — especially on WhatsApp.
✕
Avoid clicking links in job offer messages — go to the company's website directly instead. You can also paste the text or URL into VerifyJobs first.
