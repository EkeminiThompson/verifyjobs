How to Verify a Job Offer: 7 Steps Before You Apply or Pay

Most job scams collapse under basic independent checks. Use this sequence every time you receive an offer, message, or “urgent” role — especially on WhatsApp, Telegram, email, or LinkedIn.

Why verification matters

Job fraud costs victims an estimated $2 billion a year globally. Scammers copy real company names, logos, and even ATS-style pages. A polished message is not proof of a real vacancy. The steps below take minutes and prevent the two most common losses: money paid “to start” and identity documents sent too early.

The 7-step verification process

01 Search the company independently

Do not click only the links in the message. Open a new tab and search the company name plus “careers” or the city. Confirm:

  • A professional website with consistent branding and real contact details
  • Domain age that is not days or weeks old (WHOIS lookup)
  • News, reviews, or employee presence that match the claim

If the only “site” is a lookalike domain (extra letters, odd TLD), stop.

02 Find the same role on the official careers page

Legitimate openings usually appear on the employer’s own careers section or a known ATS (Greenhouse, Workday, Lever, BambooHR, Ashby). Compare title, location, and closing date. Aggregator copies and WhatsApp-only “roles” with no official listing are higher risk.

03 Inspect the recruiter’s email domain

Real companies hire from @company.com (or a clear subsidiary domain). A recruiter using Gmail, Yahoo, Hotmail, or a personal Outlook address for “official” correspondence is one of the strongest scam signals worldwide. See also our guide on 15 job scam warning signs.

04 Review LinkedIn (company + people)

Check the company page for employees and recent posts. Open the recruiter’s profile: account age, connections, mutual people, and whether they list the same employer. New profiles that only message candidates are a common pattern.

05 Refuse any upfront payment

Hard stop: Legitimate employers do not charge candidates for training, equipment, background checks, “registration,” or starter kits. Any request for money before day one is advance-fee fraud. Read the full breakdown: Job requires upfront payment? That’s almost always a scam.

07 Contact the company through a known channel

Use the official website contact form, published switchboard, or careers inbox — not the WhatsApp number or reply-to address in the message. Ask whether the vacancy and recruiter name are real. Silence or confusion is useful information.

Stop immediately if you see these

  • Any fee before you start work
  • WhatsApp- or Telegram-only hiring with no formal process
  • Requests for passport, national ID, or bank details before a real interview or written offer
  • Salaries far above market for simple remote tasks
  • Pressure to “decide today” or limited slots that vanish if you research

What to do if it fails the checks

Do not send money or sensitive documents. Screenshot the conversation and report it. Country-specific links are on our Report a scam page (Nigeria EFCC, USA FTC, UK Action Fraud, Ghana, Kenya, India, Canada, and others).
