Report a Job Scam

If you've encountered a fraudulent job posting — whether or not you lost money — reporting it protects others. Here's exactly what to do and where to go.

If you've already sent money or shared bank details, act now.

Contact your bank immediately to freeze transfers and file a fraud claim. Time is critical — the sooner you call, the better the chance of recovery.

What to Do Right Now

1
Stop all contact with the scammer

Do not reply to any further messages. Do not send any more money, documents, or information. Block their number/email after taking screenshots.

2
Screenshot everything

Take screenshots of all messages, emails, job postings, payment requests, and contact details before blocking. This is your evidence for any report or bank dispute.

3
Contact your bank if money was sent

Call your bank's fraud line immediately. Explain you sent money to a scammer. Request a recall or chargeback. If you sent via mobile money (Opay, Palmpay, MTN Mobile Money), contact their fraud teams directly.

4
Change any passwords shared

If you provided a password, email access, or logged in to any portal they sent you, change those passwords immediately and enable two-factor authentication.

5
File an official report

Use the agency links below for your country. Even if recovery seems unlikely, reports help agencies track scam networks and warn others.

Protect Yourself Going Forward

Always verify a company exists independently — search their name + "scam" and check LinkedIn, official websites, and Companies House / CAC registrations.
Call the company's official number (found on their verified website, not the one in the job posting) to confirm the recruiter works there.
Never accept a job offer without a proper interview, employment contract, or verifiable company registration.
Never pay for a job. Legitimate employers do not charge for training, starter kits, background checks, or processing fees.
Never share your BVN, NIN, passport, or bank details with a recruiter who contacted you unsolicited.
Never click links in unsolicited job offer messages — they may lead to phishing sites designed to steal your login credentials.
